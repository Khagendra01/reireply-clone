The missing piece: Channel Capabilities
This is something I'd explicitly model.

@dataclass
class ChannelCapabilities:
    supports_voice: bool
    supports_images: bool
    supports_video: bool
    supports_buttons: bool
    supports_carousel: bool
    supports_location: bool
    supports_forms: bool
    supports_file_upload: bool
    supports_typing_indicator: bool
    supports_read_receipts: bool
Then your renderers can decide how to present the same logical response.

For example, if the AI wants to present three appointment slots:

Voice: "I have openings at 10:00 AM, 1:00 PM, and 3:00 PM. Which would you like?"

SMS: "Reply 1 for 10:00 AM, 2 for 1:00 PM, or 3 for 3:00 PM."

RCS: Three tappable suggestion chips or appointment cards.

Web: Interactive buttons or a calendar picker.

The conversation engine didn't change—only the renderer.

I think this is the cleanest mental model because Voice AI and RCS stop being "features" and become first-class rendering pipelines. The AI reasons in terms of intent and semantic responses ("present three appointment options"), while the renderer for each channel decides how that intent should be experienced—spoken dialogue for voice, rich cards and carousels for RCS, plain text for SMS, or interactive UI for the web. That's what makes the same conversation engine reusable across every communication channel.
